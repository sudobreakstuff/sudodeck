Guide

From unboxing to pushing your first macro — here's how it all works.

1

Get a CYD

You need a Cheap Yellow Display (ESP32-2432S028R). They cost ~$10 on AliExpress or Amazon. Search for "ESP32 2.8 inch TFT display". Any variant with the CH340 USB-serial chip works.

Already have one? Great. Plug it into USB and skip to the next step.
2

Flash the Firmware

Your CYD comes with factory demo software. You need to replace it with SudoDeck. Enter flash mode: hold the BOOT button, tap RESET, release BOOT — the screen goes blank. Then use the firmware tool to flash.

Go to Firmware Tool
This is a one-time step unless you want to update to a newer version later.
3

Connect to the Config Tool

After flashing, the CYD boots into SudoDeck. Leave it plugged into USB (this powers the board and provides a data connection). Click Connect in the Configure section below — your browser will ask which serial port to use.

Go to Configure
Use Chrome or Edge. Web Serial requires a secure context — GitHub Pages (HTTPS) works.
4

Configure Your Layout

Once connected, the config tool reads your current layout from the device. Add pages, place buttons on the grid, pick colors, and assign actions (keyboard keys, media controls, text snippets, or combos). Hit Write to save to the CYD's flash.

The config is stored on the device. Unplug and move to another computer — your layout follows.
5

Pair via Bluetooth

Unplug the USB (or keep it plugged for power — your choice). The CYD runs on USB power. Open Bluetooth settings on your computer, phone, or tablet. Look for SudoDeck in the device list and pair. Pair once, it stays paired.

No drivers. No daemons. No app install. Just standard Bluetooth HID.
6

Use It

That's it. Push a button on the CYD — your macro fires on the paired device. Navigate pages, trigger combos, type snippets. The screensaver kicks in after a minute of inactivity (customize it in the config tool).

Powered by BLE HID — your computer thinks it's a real keyboard. Reliable, fast, zero configuration.

SudoDeck

cheap. open. yours.

SudoDeck turns a $10 CYD display into a wireless macro deck. Pair it over Bluetooth, configure it from your browser, use it anywhere. No subscriptions, no proprietary software, no vendor lock-in.

The Mission

Macro decks shouldn't cost a hundred dollars. We believe affordable tech is a right, not a privilege. SudoDeck is our answer — open hardware and open software that anyone can build, modify, and own.

Your Privacy

All configuration stays on the device. No cloud sync, no accounts, no telemetry. When you unplug SudoDeck, your layout follows — because it never left.

Open Source

MIT licensed. The firmware, the website, the config tool — everything is public. Fork it, hack it, build something new. The only limit is what you decide to build.

Your Way

Single keys, key combos, text strings, macro sequences — every button does what you want. Change labels, change colors, build multiple pages. No restrictions, no paid upgrades.

How it works

SudoDeck runs on an ESP32 microcontroller with a 2.8" TFT display. It connects to your computer as a standard Bluetooth keyboard (BLE HID) — no drivers, no daemons, no downloads. Your button layout is stored on the device's SPIFFS flash and edited through any browser that supports Web Serial.
